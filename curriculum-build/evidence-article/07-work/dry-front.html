A review of the research behind 309 learning mechanisms in an adaptive learning system

Curriculum and placement · Retention and review · Adaptation · Skill tracks and games · Engagement, accessibility and wellbeing

Compiled 25 October 2026 · citation and consistency audit 27 October 2026

Abstract

Savant is an adaptive learning app covering school subjects from preschool to grade 12, test preparation, languages and three skill tracks (chess, piano and basketball). This report sets out the research behind every rule in the app that affects how a person learns: each threshold, timing, ordering, gate, adaptation and screen behaviour, 309 mechanisms in all. For each mechanism it gives where it lives in the code, what it does with its exact numbers, the published evidence for and against it with a verbatim quote from each source, a graded verdict (strong, moderate, mixed, design choice, standard, gap or product rule), and whether the code matches the evidence.

The evidence base is 699 distinct sources: peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, official standards and regulations, and academic textbooks. Every source was checked against its DOI record or official page; 643 have a verbatim quote confirmed in the abstract, full text or page, and 56 are title-level only and never count toward a mechanism's minimum of three sources. Of the 309 mechanisms, 52 rest on strong evidence, 114 on moderate evidence, 39 on mixed evidence, and 85 are reasoned design choices whose general principle is supported but whose exact value no study has set. 15 follow a law or standard. The review also produced 51 evidence-backed improvement proposals, 33 of which were built, almost all behind individual on/off switches.

On 27 October 2026 the whole report was audited. Each of its 1,611 citation occurrences was checked against the cited source's abstract or official text: first by two independent language models, then by a stronger model in a final review, with planted errors mixed in to test the checkers (every model caught all of them). 235 occurrences were corrected, 62 of them by removing a source that did not show what it was cited for. As a result 17 mechanisms fell below three quoted sources. A second search added 23 verified replacement sources, and 4 mechanisms still rest on fewer than three quoted sources, each marked. 31 citation occurrences remain undecided, mostly because no abstract or open full text could be obtained. The report was also searched for internal contradictions, and 13 were fixed. The section "Citation and consistency audit" lists every change.

The research shows which direction is better for each choice: retrieval over rereading, spacing over massing, explanations over bare marks, mastery before moving on. It rarely sets Savant's exact numbers, and no study has tested these mechanisms working together. The report therefore marks the limits of the evidence throughout, and names the in-app experiments (a holdout group, a switch per change, outcomes on later unaided checks) that can confirm the whole system with real learners.

How to read this report

  • Mechanism labels take the form 01-M7: section 01, mechanism 7. Proposals take the form 01-P3.
  • Citations. A number in square brackets, such as [12], points to the numbered reference list at the end. The short label before it (for example roediger2006 [1]) is the label used in the working files.
  • Before and after 25 October 2026. The sections were written from the code as it stood on 25 October 2026. Changes built from this review that day are recorded on each mechanism's Status line. Where a mechanism changed, its What line describes the earlier behaviour unless it says otherwise, and the Status line gives the current behaviour.
  • Code locations (file:line) are as read on 25 October 2026; the changes may have moved lines.
