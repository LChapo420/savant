## <a id="section-07"></a>Section 07. Learning from your own material

This section covers turning a learner's own upload (notes, a syllabus, a textbook, a document) into a course: the skill outline, the prerequisite order, placement that skips what they know, difficulty tiers, the mastery bar, remediation, retrieval, interleaving, spaced review, generated questions tied to quotes, the checks on every item, and privacy. It was written on 30 October 2026 with the same method as section 06. The feature is built on branch `own-material-paths` and was proven on six test uploads (docs/OWN-MATERIAL-PROOF.md on that branch); it is not yet live.

Honest limits, up front:
- **No study tests a course built from a learner's own upload.** The sources test the parts (retrieval, spacing, mastery, adaptive placement, question generation), not this combination. 07-P3 proposes the first test.
- **The exact numbers are design choices:** the 75% mastery bar, the tier steps and the skip threshold. No study sets them for this setting.
- **Language-model evidence is recent.** The question-generation and judging sources are mostly from 2023 to 2026, and several are from medicine or general NLP, not school learning.
- **A paraphrase is printed only where two checks confirmed it.** For the other sources the section shows their own words only (see the rules at the top of this section's build script, `curriculum-build/evidence-article/07-work/build07.js`).

**In numbers:** 13 mechanisms; 13 with 3 or more quoted sources; 1 strong, 6 moderate, 2 mixed, 4 design choice; 71 distinct sources cited; 13 mechanisms built on 29-30 October 2026 (not yet live).

### 07-A. From an upload to a learning path

#### <a id="07-M1"></a>07-M1. The learner's own material becomes a skill outline

- **Where:** `services/course-builder.js:86-118` (chunk), 185-205 (cleanOutline), 320-332 (outlineWindows), 424-530 (build, outline stage); `services/materials.js:48-66, 113-128` (extraction, upload, paste)
- **Evidence:** chau2021 [653] (indexing textbook sections with concepts by hand is effortful and error-prone, which motivates automatic keyphrase/concept extraction from the material); pan2017 [654]; nesbit2006 [655] (learning with concept/knowledge maps (constructing, modifying or viewing node-link diagrams) is associated with better knowledge retention); anastasiou2024 [656]; pinandito2021 [657] (automatic concept-map generation from learning material can yield generic maps that do not adequately match the teacher's intent or learning context, which motivated a semi-automatic approach); lazonder2016 [658].
  - chau2021 [653]: "manual indexing of each textbook section with concepts is challenging, time-consuming, and prone to errors" (Digital/adaptive textbooks and their section-level knowledge models (NLP keyphrase extraction applied in a textbook context))
  - pan2017 [654]: "We study the extent to which the prerequisite relation between knowledge concepts in Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can be inferred automatically" (MOOC course concepts; three Coursera datasets)
  - nesbit2006 [655]: "the use of concept maps was associated with increased knowledge retention" (Students from Grade 4 to postsecondary in science, psychology, statistics and nursing; 55 studies, 5,818 participants)
  - anastasiou2024 [656]: "Concept maps were generally associated with increased science learning across several learning and teaching conditions" (Grade 3 to Grade 12 students in physics/earth science, chemistry and biology; 55 studies, 5,364 students)
  - pinandito2021 [657]: "automatic-generated concept maps with Concept Map Mining (CMM) can only produce general concept maps that are not fit" (EFL reading-comprehension learning with a web-based Kit-Build concept-map authoring tool; cuts against the design)
  - lazonder2016 [658]: "Results showed facilitative overall effects of guidance on learning activities" (Learners across age categories in inquiry-based learning; meta-analysis of 72 studies)
- **Verdict:** **design choice**. Concept and knowledge maps help learning (Nesbit 2006; Anastasiou 2024), and indexing material by hand is costly and error-prone (Chau 2021). Fully automatic concept maps tend to be generic (Pinandito 2021), which is why the outline is checked against the source (07-M12). No study tests this outline method. Gap: a part of the upload that fails all tries is left out without telling the learner (07-P1).
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M2"></a>07-M2. A prerequisite graph orders the levels

- **Where:** `services/course-builder.js:207-254` (orderSkills, layout); `services/material-study.js:134-138` (unlocked), 147-157 (nextStep)
- **Evidence:** white1973 [659] (in Gagné's hierarchy work, no child acquired a higher skill without first acquiring the subordinate skills shown beneath it in the hierarchy); white1973b [660] (the strong claim that prerequisites are essential holds only for intellectual skills, not for specific verbalized facts, so gating every skill on mastery is not supported across all content types); bai2025 [661] (identifying prerequisite dependencies between learning resources is an established research task aimed at organizing content and personalizing learning); manrique2019 [662] (candidate prerequisites can be found in knowledge graphs and filtered by a supervised model, validated against a ground truth of concepts from different domains); pan2017 [654] (prerequisite relations between MOOC concepts can be inferred automatically from learned concept representations, improving on prior methods); molontay2020 [663].
  - white1973 [659]: "none of them acquired a skill without also acquiring all of the skills that were shown as subordinate to it in the hierarchy" (Seven children in Gagné's original teaching study, as described in White's review of learning-hierarchy research)
  - white1973b [660]: "the strong hypothesis that there are essential prerequisites in learning and the weak hypothesis that there are helpful precursors" (Conceptual analysis of Gagné's learning-hierarchy model, dividing subject matter into general intellectual skills versus specific verbalized knowledge; cuts against the design)
  - bai2025 [661]: "Prerequisite relation (PR) learning is a fundamental task in educational technology that identifies dependencies between learning resources to facilitate personalized learning experiences" (Systematic survey of prerequisite-relation learning methods for knowledge concepts and learning objects)
  - manrique2019 [662]: "Automated systems and instructional designers evaluate and order concepts' complexity to successfully generate and recommend or adapt learning paths" (80 ground-truth concepts from different domains, using semantic web knowledge graphs and supervised learning)
  - pan2017 [654]: "Our experiments on three datasets form Coursera show that the proposed method achieves significant improvements" (Three Coursera MOOC datasets of knowledge concepts)
  - molontay2020 [663]: "It determines the learning goals of the program and also has a huge impact on completion time and dropping out." (Electrical engineering program at Budapest University of Technology and Economics; probabilistic student-flow modelling of prerequisite networks)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. Learners did not acquire higher skills without the subordinate skills in Gagne-style hierarchies (White 1973), though the strong form holds for intellectual skills, not facts. Prerequisites can be inferred automatically but imperfectly (Pan 2017; Manrique 2019), and curriculum structure affects completion (Molontay 2020). Code matches: an inferred graph orders the levels.
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M3"></a>07-M3. Placement over the skill graph skips what the learner already knows

- **Where:** `services/material-path.js:66-195` (answerModel, belief, nextPlacement, placementResult); `services/material-study.js:183-275` (placementNext, placementAnswer, finishPlacement); `services/adaptive-placement.js:66-71` (assumed)
- **Evidence:** cheng2000 [664] (trait level can be estimated with reasonable accuracy from a small number of item responses, and most CAT algorithms performed equally well once a test reached about ten items); doble2019 [665] (a large simulation using real ALEKS data examined how reliably a knowledge-space-theory adaptive assessment measures what a learner knows, showing the knowledge-space approach is being evaluated at scale rather than assumed correct); mouenis2015 [666] (bayesian networks are proposed as a probabilistic framework for dynamically updating a model of what each learner knows as they work); erylmaz2020 [667] (a fuzzy Bayesian intelligent tutoring system (FB-ITS) produced significantly higher average academic performance than fuzzy ITS, Bayesian ITS, and a traditional e-learning system, and was designed to adaptively support students in learning environments); polack2022 [668] (retrieval during testing itself can improve later performance, making testing a learning opportunity).
  - cheng2000 [664]: "most CAT algorithms performed equally well for tests of 10 or more items." (Simulated and empirical CAT response data in a psychometric methods study)
  - doble2019 [665]: "A large-scale simulation study of the assessment effectiveness of a particular instantiation of knowledge space theory is described." (Over 700,000 actual ALEKS mathematics assessments, resimulated)
  - mouenis2015 [666]: "to use Bayesian networks as a probabilistic framework to resolve the issue of dynamic management and update of the learner model" (Learner modeling in adaptive educational hypermedia systems (book chapter, no controlled learner sample))
  - erylmaz2020 [667]: "students who studied using FB-ITS had significantly higher academic performance on average compared to other students who studied with the other systems." (120 undergraduate university students using an intelligent tutoring system)
  - polack2022 [668]: "the additional processing that occurs during testing has been shown to be relevant for future performance." (Review of the testing effect across tasks and parameters in the learning literature; cuts against the design)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. Adaptive testing estimates a level from few answers (Cheng 2000), Bayesian learner models support it (Tadlaoui 2015; Eryilmaz 2020), and knowledge-space placement was examined at scale (Doble 2019). Against: testing is itself practice, so skipping what is judged known removes some retrieval (Polack 2022). Code matches; the skip threshold is a design choice.
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M4"></a>07-M4. Difficulty tiers inside a skill, harder after mastery

- **Where:** `services/material-path.js:196-237` (updatePace, startTier, practiceCount, tierAfter, pickForTier); `services/material-study.js:278-305, 323-344, 374-466` (lesson, pickQuestions, submit)
- **Evidence:** tetzlaff2025 [61] (a meta-analysis suggests an expertise reversal effect: learners with low prior knowledge learn more from high-assistance instruction, whereas learners with high prior knowledge learn more from low-assistance instruction; this suggests that reducing heavy support as prior knowledge grows may be appropriate, although the meta-analysis did not evaluate the app); kalyuga2003 [6] (instructional support that helps novices can lose effectiveness or backfire for more knowledgeable learners, which is the general principle behind reducing guidance at higher tiers); leppink2012 [669] (in a statistics course, low prior knowledge students gained most from worked examples while high prior knowledge students gained most from generating their own arguments, illustrating a shift in optimal support with prior knowledge); lazonder2016 [658]; nelson2023 [670] (difficulties that demand more effort may impede short-term learning but may be beneficial for long-term learning); dobson2011 [671].
  - tetzlaff2025 [61]: "Low prior knowledge learners learn better from high-assistance instruction (d = 0.505)." (Meta-analysis of 60 experimental studies, 176 effect sizes, 5924 participants (learning-instruction research))
  - kalyuga2003 [6]: "Instructional techniques that are highly effective with inexperienced learners can lose their effectiveness and even have negative consequences when used with more experienced learners." (Review of empirical studies on learner expertise and instructional techniques (mainly cognitive load / worked-example research))
  - leppink2012 [669]: "low prior knowledge students learn most from studying worked-out examples, whereas high prior knowledge students profit most from formulating arguments" (University students learning statistics, split by low vs high prior knowledge)
  - lazonder2016 [658]: "Research has consistently shown that inquiry-based learning can be more effective than other, more expository instructional approaches as long as students are supported adequately." (Meta-analysis of 72 studies comparing types of guidance across age categories; cuts against the design)
  - nelson2023 [670]: "Difficulties that require more effort from learners may impede short-term learning but are ultimately beneficial for long-term learning" (Health professions trainees; review synthesizing psychology, cognitive science and medical education evidence)
  - dobson2011 [671]: "There were no significant differences between the blocked and interleaved groups on any of the assessments" (189 students studying physiology passages (immunology and reproductive physiology); cuts against the design)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. The expertise reversal effect is supported by a meta-analysis (Tetzlaff 2025; Kalyuga 2003; Leppink 2011): support that helps novices can hinder learners who know more, which is what harder tiers after mastery do. Guidance generally helps (Lazonder 2016), and not every added difficulty pays off (Dobson 2011). Code matches.
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M5"></a>07-M5. Mastery before moving on (75%)

- **Where:** `services/material-path.js:34` (PASS); `services/material-study.js:346-466` (startQuiz, submit)
- **Evidence:** kulik1990 [4] (a meta-analysis of 108 controlled evaluations found that mastery learning programmes have positive effects on examination performance across college, high school and upper elementary students, with effects that appear stronger for weaker students); slavin1990 [672] (a best-evidence synthesis of group-based mastery learning in schools concluded that, in studies lasting at least four weeks, mastery learning had essentially no effect on standardized achievement tests and only a small effect on researcher-made tests, which may be biased toward the treatment); pelanek2018 [673] (an analysis of mastery criteria with simulated and real data found that which data are used for the mastery decision and where the threshold is set matter more than the learner-modelling technique); changeiywo2008 [674] (a quasi-experimental study (Solomon four-group design) compared a mastery learning approach with regular teaching for a three-week physics topic in Kenyan secondary schools); post2023 [675] (in a virtual-reality abdominal ultrasound course, 21 of 24 trainees reached a predefined mastery level on two consecutive attempts, and the study set out to measure the cost per trainee of reaching that standard).
  - kulik1990 [4]: "mastery learning programs have positive effects on the examination performance of students in colleges, high schools, and the upper grades in elementary schools" (Students in colleges, high schools, and upper elementary grades (108 controlled evaluations))
  - slavin1990 [672]: "in studies of at least 4 weeks' duration, mastery learning had essentially no effect on standardized achievement measures and a small effect on experimenter-made measures" (Elementary and secondary school students in group-based mastery learning studies; cuts against the design)
  - pelanek2018 [673]: "the choice of data sources used for mastery decision and the setting of thresholds are more important than the choice of a learner modelling technique" (Simulated and real learner data from an educational practice system (single knowledge component))
  - changeiywo2008 [674]: "The study was Quasi-experimental and Solomon Four Non-equivalent Control Group Design was used." (161 Form Two students in four co-educational secondary schools, Kieni East Division, Nyeri District, Kenya)
  - post2023 [675]: "Twenty-one out of the 24 trainees managed to attain the predefined mastery level two times consecutively." (Trainees practising general abdominal ultrasound on a virtual reality simulator (24 trainees))
- **Verdict:** **mixed**. Mastery learning raised exam performance in a meta-analysis (Kulik 1990), but a synthesis of longer school studies found little effect (Slavin 1990), and the mastery criterion itself matters (Pelanek 2018). The 75% bar is a design choice (07-P2).
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M6"></a>07-M6. Remediation at the misconception behind a wrong answer

- **Where:** `services/course-builder.js:142-171` (SKILL_SCHEMA misconceptions), 279-318 (cleanQuestion); `services/material-study.js:85-90` (misconceptionOf), 278-305 (lesson remediation), 420-462 (submit)
- **Evidence:** vanderkleij2015 [15] (in computer-based learning, elaborated feedback with an explanation produced larger effects than feedback only on answer correctness); swart2019 [676] (when feedback on text learning is given directly after reading, elaborate feedback was more effective than knowledge-of-response feedback); tamir1990 [677] (multiple-choice item structure, when used wisely, can serve as a diagnostic tool for students' conceptions, including misconceptions); broughton2010 [678]; palmer2003 [679]; maskiewicz2013 [680] (this essay warns that the term misconception is tied to eradication or replacement and argues that students' naive ideas can be resources for building understanding).
  - vanderkleij2015 [15]: "elaborated feedback (EF; e.g., providing an explanation) produced larger effect sizes (0.49) than feedback regarding the correctness of the answer" (Students in computer-based learning environments; meta-analysis of 40 studies and 70 effect sizes)
  - swart2019 [676]: "If feedback is provided directly after reading, elaborate feedback and knowledge-of-correct-response feedback were more effective than knowledge-of-response feedback." (Conventional readers from primary school to university; meta-analysis of 104 contrasts and 6,124 participants)
  - tamir1990 [677]: "their structure, when wisely used, makes them an excellent diagnostic tool for identifying students’ conceptions, including misconceptions." (Multiple-choice items in science education; guidelines and examples, no sample reported)
  - broughton2010 [678]: "The refutation text group had fewer misconceptions at posttest." (Undergraduates reading about seasonal change)
  - palmer2003 [679]: "Surprisingly, both texts were able to induce accommodation in a large proportion of the students." (36 grade 9 students with a misconception about ecological role; mixed)
  - maskiewicz2013 [680]: "students' naïve ideas can provide the resources from which to build scientific understanding." (Biology education researchers and the learning sciences community; essay using evolution and ecology examples; cuts against the design)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. Elaborated feedback beats knowledge of results (van der Kleij 2015; Swart 2019), multiple-choice distractors can diagnose conceptions (Tamir 1990), and refutation texts reduce misconceptions (Broughton 2010). Mixed results (Palmer 2003) and a critique of the misconception frame (Maskiewicz 2013) argue for building on what the learner already thinks. Code matches.
- **Status:** Built on branch `own-material-paths`, 29-30 October 2026; not yet merged into the live app.

#### <a id="07-M7"></a>07-M7. Retrieval practice with feedback

- **Where:** `services/material-study.js:278-321` (lesson, practice), 346-466 (startQuiz, submit)
- **Evidence:** adesope2017 [11]; agarwal2021 [99]; schwieren2017 [612]; moreira2019 [681]; goossens2016 [682] (in regular primary-school vocabulary lessons, retrieval practice exercises showed no retention advantage over copying the word description); racsmany2020 [683] (feedback given at final recall can reverse the usual testing advantage, at least when practice retrieval success was low).
  - adesope2017 [11]: "Results reveal that practice tests are more beneficial for learning than restudying and all other comparison conditions." (Meta-analysis pooling experiments with varied learners, materials and settings)
  - agarwal2021 [99]: "retrieval practice improved learning for a variety of education levels, content areas, experimental designs, final test delays" (50 classroom experiments, total n = 5,374, mostly in WEIRD countries)
  - schwieren2017 [612]: "A significant overall effect size of d = 0.56 demonstrated that testing was beneficial to the learning outcomes." (19 publications, 72 effect sizes, university psychology classes)
  - moreira2019 [681]: "their results are favorable to the use of retrieval practice in classroom settings, regardless of whether feedback is provided or not" (Classroom studies from elementary school children to medical school students; mixed)
  - goossens2016 [682]: "Both on the cued‐recall test and on the multiple‐choice test no benefits of long‐lag repetition and retrieval practice were found." (Grades 2, 3, 4 and 6 children doing exercises in regular L2 vocabulary lessons; cuts against the design)
  - racsmany2020 [683]: "a feedback-induced reversed testing effect was present only at low retrieval success during practice" (Three laboratory experiments manipulating retrieval success; learner population not specified in the abstract; cuts against the design)
- **Verdict:** **strong**. Practice tests beat restudy across many experiments (Adesope 2017) and in real classrooms (Agarwal 2021; Schwieren 2017). Exceptions exist (Goossens 2016; Racsmany 2020). Code matches.

#### <a id="07-M8"></a>07-M8. Interleaving earlier skills into new checks and mixing reviews

- **Where:** `services/material-study.js:323-344` (pickQuestions), 440-450 (interleaved result handling)
- **Evidence:** rohrer2020 [13] (in a preregistered cluster randomized trial in real classrooms, students given interleaved assignments outscored blocked-practice students on an unannounced test one month later); rohrer2015 [12] (seventh-graders who practiced the same problems in interleaved rather than blocked order learned the skills over a 3-month period); brunmair2019 [14] (the interleaving advantage is larger when the material being mixed is more similar across categories, so what gets mixed together matters); firth2021 [163] (interleaving helped most when differences between the mixed items were subtle, and the benefit extended to delayed tests); samani2021 [684] (undergraduates who did interleaved physics homework recalled more and solved more problems correctly on surprise tests, though they judged the method harder and thought they learned less); vanpeppen2021 [685] (in critical-thinking practice, there interleaved practice showed no indications of better learning or transfer than blocked practice, irrespective of task format).
  - rohrer2020 [13]: "the interleaved group outscored the blocked group, 61% versus 38%, d = 0.83" (54 seventh-grade mathematics classes in a naturalistic school setting, 4 months of assignments, unannounced test 1 month later)
  - rohrer2015 [12]: "126 seventh-grade students received the same practice problems over a 3-month period, but the problems were arranged so that skills were learned by interleaved practice" (126 seventh-grade students, 3-month practice period with an unannounced test 1 or 30 days after a review session)
  - brunmair2019 [14]: "A multiple metaregression analysis revealed stronger interleaving effects for learning material more similar between categories" (Meta-analysis of interleaved learning experiments (mainly inductive category learning tasks))
  - firth2021 [163]: "Interleaving was found to be of greatest use when differences between items are subtle" (Systematic review of 26 studies (17 in a meta-analysis), dominated by laboratory studies of university undergraduates)
  - samani2021 [684]: "students recalled more relevant information and more frequently produced correct solutions after having engaged in interleaved practice" (Undergraduates in two lecture sections of an introductory university physics course, 8 weeks of homework, surprise criterial tests)
  - vanpeppen2021 [685]: "there were no indications that interleaved practice led to better learning/transfer than blocked practice, irrespective of task format" (Two experiments on avoiding biased reasoning: laboratory (N = 85) and classroom (N = 117) students; cuts against the design)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. Interleaving beat blocking in a preregistered classroom trial in mathematics (Rohrer 2020; Rohrer 2015) and helps most when items are similar (Brunmair 2019; Firth 2021). It did not help critical-thinking practice (van Peppen 2021). Code matches.

#### <a id="07-M9"></a>07-M9. Spaced review timed to this learner's forgetting

- **Where:** `services/material-path.js:238-256` (recall, intervalDays, updateForgetting); `services/scorecard/forgetting.js:88-110, 308-314` (predict, recallAt); `services/material-study.js:139-145` (scheduleReview), 413-432 (review grading)
- **Evidence:** cepeda2006 [22]; cepeda2008 [23] (spacing improves long-term retention, but the optimal gap changes with the test delay rather than being a single fixed rule); settles2016 [25] (a trainable half-life regression model can estimate memory half-life and predict student recall better than baselines); tabibian2019 [26] (under a recall-maximization-with-review-cost model, the optimal review time can be set by the recall probability itself); lindsey2014 [27] (personalized review improved course retention over massed study in a middle-school foreign-language course); khajah2014 [260] (computational memory models can give practical guidance on spacing review under a fixed weekly review-time budget).
  - cepeda2006 [22]: "Analyses suggest that ISI and retention interval operate jointly to affect final-test retention; specifically, the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased." (Meta-analysis of 317 experiments in 184 articles on verbal recall tasks)
  - cepeda2008 [23]: "At any given test delay, an increase in the interstudy gap at first increased, and then gradually reduced, final test performance." (More than 1,350 individuals learning facts, with review up to 3.5 months later and final test up to 1 year later)
  - settles2016 [25]: "reducing error by 45%+ compared to several baselines at predicting student recall rates." (Duolingo online language-learning users)
  - tabibian2019 [26]: "the optimal reviewing schedule is given by the recall probability itself." (Theoretical memory models plus a large-scale natural experiment using Duolingo data)
  - lindsey2014 [27]: "personalized review yielded a 16.5% boost in course retention over current educational practice (massed study)" (Middle-school foreign-language students over a semester, tested with a cumulative exam after the semester)
  - khajah2014 [260]: "we turn to two established models of memory-ACT-R and MCM-to conduct simulation studies exploring the impact of study schedule on long-term retention." (Simulation studies using the ACT-R and MCM memory models for educational review scheduling)
- **Verdict:** **moderate**. Spacing improves retention, with the best gap depending on the retention interval (Cepeda 2006; 2008). Per-learner review timing from a memory model beat massed study in a school language course (Lindsey 2014), and half-life models predict recall (Settles 2016). This exact forgetting update is a design choice. Code matches.

#### <a id="07-M10"></a>07-M10. Questions and lessons generated from the learner's text by a language model

- **Where:** `services/course-builder.js:354-413` (buildSkill), 272-277 (shuffleChoices)
- **Evidence:** kurdi2020 [134] (a review of 93 educational automatic question generation papers finds that manual item writing is costly and slow, which is the practical motivation for machine-generated questions); wang2025 [686] (adding a teacher-derived prompt pattern to an LLM produced high-quality questions that expert evaluators scored significantly better and that approached teacher-written items in some respects); mistry2024 [687] (radiologists blind to source rated GPT-4 board-style items highly on clarity, relevance and distractor quality, though the study evaluated items, not learning gains); ahmed2024 [688]; gierl2017 [360]; carnegie2017 [689] (the study tested whether four to five consecutive MCQs with the same correct-answer letter in computer-scrambled exam versions affected undergraduates’ ability to continue selecting the correct answer, and found no adverse effect on performance).
  - kurdi2020 [134]: "manual construction of questions is a complex process that requires training, experience, and resources" (93 educational AQG papers published 2015 to early 2019 (systematic review, not a learner trial))
  - wang2025 [686]: "integrating a prompt pattern with a collective knowledge base into Large Language Models (LLMs) results in high-quality questions with statistically significant results" (Expert evaluations of LLM-generated questions built from a teacher knowledge base (unspecified learners))
  - mistry2024 [687]: "9.9 (±0.4), 9.9 (±0.5), 9.9 (±0.4), 9.8 (±0.5), and 9.9 (±0.3), respectively, for GPT-4" (104 radiology board-style MCQs from Llama 2 and GPT-4, rated by two board-certified radiologists)
  - ahmed2024 [688]: "There were no significant differences between the questions generated by ChatGPT and Bard." (64 dental caries MCQs generated from 16 book paragraphs, assessed by three dental specialists; mixed)
  - gierl2017 [360]: "summarize earlier research on the optimal number of distractors and the optimal ordering of distractors" (Multiple-choice testing literature in education (comprehensive review))
  - carnegie2017 [689]: "This project investigated a possible influence of correct answer organization on student answer selection when writing multiple versions of MCQ exams." (First- and second-year undergraduate courses with large-class MCQ exams)
- **Verdict:** **mixed**. Hand-written items are costly and automatic generation is an established field (Kurdi 2020). Expert reviewers rated LLM-generated questions highly in two studies (Wang 2024; Mistry 2024), but one found problems with some items (Ahmed 2024). That is why every item is tied to a quote (07-M11) and checked (07-M12).

#### <a id="07-M12"></a>07-M12. A second model checks every item before a learner sees it, and independent checkers audit the result

#### <a id="07-M13"></a>07-M13. Privacy and deletion of uploaded material

- **Where:** `services/materials.js:96-140` (add, remove); `services/course-builder.js:554-557` (removeForMaterial); `services/typed-grading.js:579-592` (outsideAllowed); `routes/materials.js` (owner checks, child upload gate)
- **Evidence:** drachsler2016 [311]; jones2020 [695]; mutimukwe2022 [696] (students' perceived privacy risk is a firm predictor of their privacy concerns; perceived privacy control and privacy risk determine trusting beliefs, while privacy concerns and perceived privacy risk affect non-self-disclosure behaviours); prinsloo2022 [697] (in learning analytics, technical privacy measures rest on assumptions about individual control and depend on institutional governance, consent, data security, and accountability, not technology alone); adams2023 [698] (ethics guidance specifically for children and K-12 AI has lagged behind general AI ethics policy and draws on many core general principles, while also introducing some principles unique to K-12 education); leatongray2020 [699] (children's data privacy rights under GDPR are raised as a hard problem, with current regulation not easily supporting a fair balance).
  - drachsler2016 [311]: "We conclude the paper with an eight-point checklist named DELICATE that can be applied by teachers, researchers, policy makers" (Learning analytics in education; addressed to teachers, researchers, policy makers and institutional managers)
  - jones2020 [695]: "Institutions rarely inform their students about LA practices, and there exist significant privacy concerns." (Over 100 undergraduate students interviewed at eight U.S. higher education institutions)
  - mutimukwe2022 [696]: "They also illustrate that students' perceived privacy risk is a firm predictor of their privacy concerns." (132 students at three Swedish universities, survey study in higher education)
  - prinsloo2022 [697]: "many technological solutions are based on assumptions, such as that individuals have control over their data" (Conceptual analysis of learning analytics privacy in (higher) education, critical data studies perspective; cuts against the design)
  - adams2023 [698]: "we examine the ethical principles currently informing AI ethics policy development for children and K-12 education" (K-12 education; analysis of four international AI-in-education ethics guideline statements)
  - leatongray2020 [699]: "This in turn is used to highlight data privacy rights issues for children and young people" (Children and young people in schools using AI education systems, UK/GDPR context; mixed)
- **Verdict:** **design choice**. Learning analytics needs explicit, checkable privacy safeguards (Drachsler 2016); students want to know what is collected and to control it (Jones 2020; Mutimukwe 2022); and children's data needs particular care (Gray 2020; Adams 2023). Technical controls alone are not enough (Prinsloo 2022). Code matches: per-learner storage, the file kept only until its text is extracted, real deletion.
