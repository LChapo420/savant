## 3. Method and counts

Methods are listed cheapest and most reliable first.

1. **Code checks** (authoritative where they apply). They ran on every unit, in `fact-audit/codecheck.js` and `keyalign.js`:
   - **Multiple-choice structure, all 4,255 questions.** The key must appear exactly once and the options must be distinct. 15 game questions failed, and all 15 are confirmed.
   - **Math.** A linear-equation, substitution and arithmetic solver checked 234 math keys and 766 arithmetic statements in lessons, explanations and the content cache. No wrong keys were found. The 7 arithmetic hits were deliberate "wrong work" examples, which are correct as written.
     - **Limit:** the solver did not catch the lesson 233 check question. Its prompt states no equation to solve, so the solver had nothing to work on; a judge caught it.
   - **Chess, piano and basketball checkers** (`services/*-checks.js`) on the 3 skill block plans and their checks and facts: 0 problems.
   - **Key-alignment heuristic**, which tests whether the explanation's words point to a different option than the key: 398 flagged.
2. **Retrieval** (offline Wikipedia and Wiktionary through kiwix, then FactCG and MiniCheck stage 1). Run on the 48 fact-pool facts and 12 Spanish vocabulary keys: 8 of 60 were supported.
   - **Two of the "supported" matches came from weak sources.** "hola" matched Portuguese "olá", and the piano's black-key groups matched the Wicki–Hayden layout article.
   - **"Unsupported" is not evidence of an error.** No fact-pool fact or vocabulary key was found wrong by any method. Retrieval cannot reach claims about a lesson's own examples, which is where the errors are.
3. **Judges** (free, never shown learners' words):
   - **NVIDIA Nemotron Ultra (medium).**
     - Whole-unit reads: 150 of 155 (120 lessons and 30 block plans).
     - Question review: 40 lesson checks.
     - Blind answers: 30.
     - It ran until 18:40, when Ultra started returning 429 on the probe. I stopped all NVIDIA calls then.
   - **xKiro Qwen3.8 Max.**
     - A random calibration sample: 300 questions, both reviewed with keys and answered blind, plus 10 lessons.
     - The 98 key-alignment flags with the strongest signal, reviewed.
     - 344 questions reviewed in total, under its own stop line of 444K free tokens a day.
4. **My check of every judge "wrong".** Each was checked against the linked source:
   - algebra by working the equation;
   - grammar and usage by Wikipedia or Wiktionary;
   - game keys also against the question's own explanation.

**Nemotron's typical false alarms:**

- Reading "adding 7 instead of subtracting 7 ... gives 22" literally. The common-mistake wording there is fine; it only becomes false when the text says "to both sides".
- Calling a true but simplified rule wrong.
- Misquoting the text.

**Qwen's false alarms:**

- One arithmetic claim. Lesson cache row 9 really does give 11, −4 and 5.
- Usage matters where a key is defensible, such as an ellipsis versus a dash for hesitation.

**No "wrong" was ever applied automatically.** That matches the earlier finding that free judges make false "wrong" calls.
