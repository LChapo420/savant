## Made-for-kids / safety notes
- Audience is children: YouTube and TikTok may treat it as made-for-kids (comments off, no personalised ads, fewer reach tools). Decide per platform; do not mislabel. Kids' videos still need a parent or teacher to share, so write for the adult co-viewer too (nostalgia, "this is so my class").
- Keep scenes gentle: nothing scary, no real teachers, schools or names, no asking kids for personal details. Comment prompts go to parents/teachers or are simple opinion questions.
- The factory writes its own script: CHECK each voiceover for invented facts (marked FACT-CHECK) and regenerate lines that turn dark or preachy.

## Common pattern to copy
One personal, second-person premise ("what if YOUR brain..."), a real fact as the turn at about 10 s, a rule that gets worse step by step (the Higgsfield sample pattern), a closing line that reframes the viewer's own head. Length 20 to 40 s so retention can stay high. Preset: no link found; pick the factory's closest "science / what-if explainer" preset in the library, or leave blank.

## 1. Forgetting stops (confidence: medium-high)
- What-if line: `What if your brain stopped forgetting, one day at a time, until you remembered everything?`
- Length 30 s. Preset: science what-if.
- First line / on-screen 1.5 s: "Forgetting is the only thing keeping you sane." Text: YOU STOP FORGETTING.
- Beats: day 1 you remember every dull meal; day 2 every awkward moment replays; day 3 you cannot tell now from then; fact turn: a few people with highly superior autobiographical memory exist, and they say it is tiring more than magical; end: forgetting is a feature your brain runs on purpose.
- Ending: "Which memory would you delete first?" (comment driver).
- Caption: "Your brain deletes things on purpose. Here is why." #brain #psychology #memory #whatif #neuroscience
- Check: HSAM is real and rare (documented since McGaugh and colleagues, 2006); do not say "photographic memory" or give a headcount.
- Evidence: RESEARCH Loewenstein 1994 (personal info gap); RESEARCH Berger and Milkman 2012 (anxiety/awe arousal); JUDGMENT: universal, second-person, dread plus awe, each beat escalates like the Higgsfield light sample.
- Flop: becomes a generic "memory tips" lecture.

## 2. You never sleep again (medium-high)
- What-if line: `What if you stayed awake for 11 days, like the real teen who tried it in 1964?`
- Use instead: `What if a person tried to never sleep, and the brain kept score, hour by hour?`
- Length 35 s. First line: "Hour 17: your brain already performs like it is drunk." Text: HOUR 17.
- Beats: hour 17 to 19 (reaction time like mild alcohol impairment, Dawson and Reid 1997, Nature); hour 24 micro-sleeps; day 2 to 3 hallucination reports; the brain forces sleep anyway; end: sleep is when the brain cleans itself (Xie et al. 2013, Science, mice).
- Ending: "How long since you slept properly?" Share to a friend who pulls all-nighters.
- Caption: "Hour by hour without sleep." #sleep #brain #psychology #neuroscience #whatif
- Check: Dawson and Reid 1997 compared 17 to 19 h awake to about 0.05% blood alcohol on performance tasks; cleaning finding is from mice. No medical advice or dosing.
- Evidence: RESEARCH Berger and Milkman 2012; MEASURED none; JUDGMENT: countdown format has a built-in reason to stay.
- Flop: reads as a health scare or lecture; keep it factual and non-graphic.

## 3. You use only 10% of your brain, and it were true (medium)
- What-if line: `What if the myth were true and you really used only 10% of your brain?`
- Length 25 s. First line: "You have heard this since school. It is false." Text: THE 10% MYTH.
- Beats: imagine the 10% version of you; damage to almost any small area causes a noticeable loss; scans show activity across the whole brain over a day; the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of energy; end: a brain mostly idle would not have been kept by evolution.
- Ending: "What did school tell you that was wrong?"
- Caption: "The oldest brain myth, killed." #brainmyths #psychology #neuroscience #didyouknow #whatif
- Check: must be framed as a MYTH throughout; verify the 2% and 20% figures in the voiceover (commonly cited, check before posting).
- Evidence: JUDGMENT: myth-busting plus comment bait ("I believed that"); RESEARCH Loewenstein 1994. Confidence medium because it is a well-worn topic.
- Flop: so familiar viewers swipe at second 1; the "you believed this" hook must be sharp.

## 4. Your inner voice goes silent (medium)
- What-if line: `What if the voice in your head went silent for one day?`
- Length 25 s. First line: "Some people have never heard an inner voice." Text: NO INNER VOICE.
- Beats: you read this silently, with a voice; some people report thinking in images or no words; you try to plan your day silently; end: nobody knows what another mind sounds like.
- Ending: "Do YOU have one? Comment yes or no." (strong comment prompt).
- Caption: "Do you have an inner monologue?" #innervoice #psychology #brain #mind #whatif
- Check: inner-speech differences are real and varied (the term anendophasia is used in research; say "some people report"); no diagnosis, no "disorder" language.
- Evidence: JUDGMENT: binary question that splits the comments, identity-based; low-cost to engage. No measured comparable found.
- Flop: voiceover overclaims percentages; ask the factory to avoid any number.

## 5. Every memory is rewritten when you recall it (medium)
- What-if line: `What if every memory you replay gets slightly rewritten, and you never find out?`
- Length 35 s. First line: "Your clearest memory may be partly invented." Text: IS YOUR MEMORY REAL?
- Beats: recall as rebuilding, not playback; Loftus and Palmer 1974: wording of a question ("smashed" vs "hit") changed estimated speed in witnesses; each replay is a rebuild; end: confidence is not accuracy.
- Ending: "Think of a childhood memory. Now doubt it." Rewatch bait.
- Caption: "Your memories are edited every time." #memory #psychology #brain #falsememory #whatif
- Check: Loftus and Palmer 1974 (J. Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior) is real; keep "can change", not "always change".
- Evidence: RESEARCH Loftus and Palmer 1974; RESEARCH Berger and Milkman 2012 (unease plus awe); JUDGMENT: personal doubt hook.
- Flop: gets abstract; keep one concrete example per beat.

## 6. Everyone gets 3 IQ points smarter every ten years (medium-low)
- What-if line: `What if average IQ scores kept rising 3 points every decade, like they did for most of the 1900s?`
- Length 30 s. First line: "Your great-grandparents would score low on today's tests." Text: IQ KEPT RISING.
- Beats: the Flynn effect (Flynn 1984, Psychological Bulletin) is a real rise in raw test scores across the 20th century; why debated (nutrition, schooling, test familiarity); some countries now show flat or falling scores; end: tests measure something, but it moves with the world.
- Ending: "What do you think changed?" (debate comments).
- Caption: "The Flynn effect, explained." #iq #flynneffect #psychology #intelligence #brain
- Check: voiceover must not say "people got genetically smarter" or "your IQ can be raised by X". Do not state exact decade figures beyond "about 3 points per decade, widely reported". Avoid claiming ancestors were unintelligent.
- Evidence: RESEARCH Flynn 1984; JUDGMENT: IQ is a high-curiosity word and fits the account's theme. Medium-low: it is a debate topic that can drift into controversy.
- Flop: reads as a claim about groups of people; keep it about eras, not people.

## 7. Your brain stops predicting (medium-low)
- What-if line: `What if you could suddenly tickle yourself?`
- Length 20 s. First line: "You cannot tickle yourself. Here is why." Text: WHY NO SELF-TICKLE.
- Beats: your brain predicts your own touch and turns the sensation down (Blakemore, Wolpert and Frith 1998, Nature Neuroscience); a delayed touch (robot) can feel tickly; end: your brain is guessing every second.
- Ending: "Try it. Then tell me." Comment prompt.
- Caption: "Your brain predicts you. #brain #neuroscience #psychology #didyouknow #whatif"
- Check: the delayed-robot-touch study is Blakemore et al. 1999 (same lab line); keep the claim to "reduces the sensation".
- Evidence: RESEARCH Blakemore et al. 1998; JUDGMENT: an easy, short, interactive fact; weaker virality ceiling.
- Flop: short and light; may lack the dread/awe arousal.

## 8. Your brain cleans itself only while you sleep (medium-low)
- What-if line: `What if your brain's night shift never clocked in?`
- Length 25 s. First line: "Your brain takes out its own trash, but only at night." Text: THE NIGHT SHIFT.
- Beats: the fluid-flow system (Xie 2013, Science, in mice); waste builds up awake; sleep opens space; end: sleep is maintenance, not a break.
- Ending: "Send this to someone who sleeps 4 hours."
- Caption: "Sleep is cleaning day." #sleep #brain #neuroscience #health #whatif
- Check: findings come from mice; human evidence is still developing. Voiceover must not say it prevents or causes any disease.
- Evidence: RESEARCH Xie 2013; JUDGMENT: highly shareable to a specific person. Overlaps idea 2 so post only one of them if the batch needs variety.
- Flop: health-scare tone; mouse caveat dropped.

## 9. You lose the ability to recognize faces (low-medium)
- What-if line: `What if you woke up unable to recognize any face, even your own mother's?`
- Length 30 s. First line: "Some people live like this every day." Text: FACE BLINDNESS.
- Beats: your brain has face-tuned regions; prosopagnosia is real and some people are born with it; they use voices, hair, walk; end: recognition is a skill you never knew you had.
- Ending: "Would you recognize your friend in a crowd?"
- Caption: "Face blindness, explained gently." #prosopagnosia #brain #psychology #neuroscience #whatif
- Check: do not name a prevalence number unless verified (studies report about 2%, check); respectful tone, no mockery, no diagnosis of viewer.
- Evidence: JUDGMENT only. Sensitive-adjacent; kept low.
- Flop: viewers feel it is about a condition rather than their own brain; sad tone lowers sharing (Berger and Milkman, low-arousal sadness).

## 10. Your brain never got bored (low-medium)
- What-if line: `What if boredom disappeared from your brain forever?`
- Length 25 s. First line: "Boredom is your brain's alarm, and it is not broken." Text: NO MORE BOREDOM.
- Beats: boredom as a signal to switch tasks; without it you never leave your phone, never daydream; mind wandering links to creative thinking in some studies (Baird et al. 2012, Psychological Science); end: boredom is the price of choosing.

- Caption: "What if boredom was gone?" #boredom #psychology #brain #focus #whatif
- Check: Baird 2012 found a modest effect on creative tasks; do not claim boredom "makes you smarter" or raises IQ.
- Evidence: JUDGMENT only; relatable but low-stakes.
- Flop: self-help tone; no hard turn.
