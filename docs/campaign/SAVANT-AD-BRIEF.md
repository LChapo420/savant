## 2. The goal and the vision

**The vision:** anyone can learn any subject, at any level, from not knowing the subject's name to being one of the most educated people in it. That includes research-level and unsolved fields.

**The goal the founder is building toward:**
- At least **4 times faster** than normal studying, for the same material, with equal or better retention.
- "Faster" means you get all the information you'd get from normal studying or a personal tutor, and you remember it for as long as you chose to. Someone who only needs it for a test date should finish fastest; someone who wants it for life still finishes faster than normal, just less so.
- An app that understands how each person learns with at least 95% accuracy within a month of use, and rebuilds everything around that.

**Why the founder built it:** they wanted to learn anything, to any depth, in the most efficient way, and keep it. They are the first user.

**These are goals, not results.** See section 6 for how to talk about them.

## 3. Why it's a big deal

School and study apps give everyone the same thing, in the same order, at the same speed. A great personal tutor doesn't: they find out what you know, skip what you've got, explain in a way that clicks for you, and come back to things before you lose them. That's among the most effective ways to learn ever measured, and most people can't afford one.

Savant's bet is that software can now do this for any subject, for anyone. What's different from a normal study app:

- **It doesn't just adapt the order. It rewrites the lesson.** Two people on the same topic get different lessons, written around their interests and pace.
- **It measures first, then teaches.** Most apps start you at lesson one or take your word for it. Savant checks.
- **It builds a picture of how you learn.** Each learner has a scorecard of 100 traits, built up from what they do and say, and it shapes what they're given. Learners see a plain-language summary.
- **It teaches by making you remember.** Recall with feedback, not rereading.
- **You choose how long to remember something:** a test date, a few months, a year, years or forever. Review is planned around that.
- **It works on your own material.** Upload your notes and get study material built from them.
- **It checks what it teaches.** The founder's standard: everything Savant makes is 100% fact-checked against credible sources before anyone sees it. Lessons are checked claim by claim. Where research is mixed, or a point is opinion, the lesson says so.

### The example on the site (usable in ads)
Same lesson, two learners, 3rd-grade math, "what 3/4 means":
- **Maya likes soccer and moves fast:** "Your team takes 4 penalty kicks and scores 3. Three out of four is 3/4." Up next: a quick game, 2 minutes.
- **Leo likes music and goes step by step:** "A bar of music has 4 beats. Clap on 3 of them. You clapped 3/4 of the bar." Up next: a worked example, 4 minutes.

This is the clearest single picture of the product: **same idea, taught two ways.**

## 4. How it works

**1. It finds your level.** A short adaptive quiz whose questions adjust to your answers. You start there and skip what you've shown you know.

**2. It builds your path.** The order and mix of lessons, games, quizzes and activities is chosen per person. One person may need one activity on a topic, another twenty.

**3. It teaches you your way.** Lessons use your interests for examples, at your level and pace. Each answer explains why it's right or wrong.

**4. You master each step.** Skills are proven before the next ones build on them, and gaps get repaired as they appear.

**5. It brings skills back just before you'd forget them.** Something slipping comes back sooner; something solid waits longer.

**6. There's a tutor beside your work.** You can ask for help as you go. On graded checks it's built to coach the method, not give the answer. A much stronger version is still being tested and isn't live, so show it as help when you're stuck and don't call it all-knowing.

## 6. What you can and can't claim (the most important section)

**Savant has no results from real learners yet.** The first real measurement starts with the beta. Every internal number so far comes from simulations and AI judges, and those aren't results.

### Never say
- **A result that hasn't happened:** "learn 4x faster", "learn in half the time", "remember twice as much", "raises your ACT by X points", "guaranteed", "proven to". This also rules out "studies show Savant...": the studies are about the methods, not Savant.
- **Fake evidence:** invented testimonials, reviews, user counts, before-and-after stories, or an AI-generated person presented as a real student or teacher. Fiction must be obviously fiction.
- **Better than a named thing:** "better than a tutor", "replaces your teacher", "the best ACT prep". Don't name competitors.
- **Perfect truth:** "100% accurate", "100% true", "never wrong". Being fact-checked and being true are different things, and no check catches everything. The founder's standard is that everything Savant makes and puts out is 100% fact-checked, so **"every lesson is fact-checked" and "100% fact-checked against credible sources" are welcome**. As on the site, say it about lessons and about the released product, and check with the founder before claiming it of the tutor or other features, since that coverage is still being finished.
- **The app knows you perfectly:** "knows exactly how you learn" or "95% accurate". Say it "learns how you learn".
- **An official link to the ACT:** no ACT, Inc. endorsement, and no "official" or "real ACT score". If you name the ACT, add: *ACT is a registered trademark of ACT, Inc., which is not affiliated with Savant.* Don't suggest it offers full-length timed practice tests in the exact ACT format.
- **Medical or diagnostic claims:** nothing diagnoses or treats ADHD, anxiety or anything else. Brain facts must be true; say "research suggests" where it's mixed.
- **The founder's age, or anything hinting at it**, such as "teen founder" or "built by a kid" (the founder's firm rule: Savant must come across as legitimate and trustworthy). Also keep out school history, personal finances, and any mention of the founder's own test scores or college plans.
- **Features that aren't there:** voice tutor, iPhone or Android apps, study groups, coding or art, other app languages, credentials.

### Always fine
- Describing what it does, in section 3 and 4 terms. "It finds your level, then teaches from there."
- The goal, framed as a goal: "We're trying to find out how much faster people can learn when every lesson is built for them. That's what the beta is for." Keep any number out of ads unless the founder approves that exact wording.
- The problem, stated as boldly as you like: rereading doesn't work well, school moves at one speed, most people can't afford a tutor. Keep the facts true.
- Honest invitations: "We're looking for people to test this." "It's early, things will break, and your feedback is the point." This honesty works in Savant's favour.
- Clearly fictional stories (Zero the number, a highlighter who lies), as long as nothing implies a real result.

### Children
- The ACT check and beta are for 13+. An under-13 needs a parent: a parent applies, using their own email. The site never asks for a child's email.
- **Don't ask kids to sign up, click or apply.** Anything aimed at younger kids must be kid-safe (no romance, no cruel bullying, no scary content) with any ask aimed at parents. Whether that account is marked "made for kids" is still the founder's decision, so raise it before posting.

## 9. What the founder wants from short videos

The founder runs Savant's channels through a content factory and posts faceless, AI-generated videos to TikTok, Reels and Shorts. The channels so far cover brand, brain and curiosity, study, younger kids, a subject account (ACT or math), and building in public.

**What they've told me they want:**
- **Original ideas.** Not copies of already viral topics: "people who've seen those will just skip mine." They rejected lists that copied existing hits.
- **A very high bar.** They rejected lists that weren't viral enough. Proof means 500k+ views, ideally 1M+, from recent videos in the same format, and made faceless or by AI (not a person on camera). Nobody can promise a video goes viral, so don't.
- **Not only what-ifs.** The factory makes more than that.
- They have their own ideas and want mass ideation with you; build on theirs.

**What was found on TikTok (Nov 2026, video pages; a sample; check before relying on it):**
- **Absurd 3D what-ifs:** 2.9M views from an account with 2,929 followers ("What if the ocean was made of..."). The best sign a new account can do this.
- **Character soaps labelled "Part 1":** a lovable non-human underdog, a humiliation, a reversal, a cliffhanger. 5.3M and 5.0M.
- **"Why you..." brain and psychology:** 15.7M (a 13-second countdown: procrastination moves the stress to the last night), 9.1M, 5.6M, 5.3M. Most run 60 to 70 seconds, open on the viewer, and answer something you've noticed but can't explain.
- **Interactive:** "Count the F's in this sentence", "What's your answer?". 1.2M to 2.8M.
- **Not found at 1M:** AI videos set in school, or building in public. Those formats are unproven.
- **Risk:** a viral video can bring followers who'd never apply. Watch applicants per view.

**Channels that failed:** Reddit (posts removed as self-promotion) and cold posting in Discord servers (about 11 hours, 0 applicants). Don't suggest either. The founder will email companies and creators themself, after testing the app, so that's not your job either.
