The evidence behind Savant

Every part of Savant is built on learning research. Here are the main ideas, in plain words, with some of the best-known studies behind each one.

This is only a small part of our research. Behind this page is a full review of every one of the hundreds of mechanisms in Savant, backed by over 500 verified studies and official standards: more than 20 times the sources shown here. We are keeping that work private until Savant launches, so it cannot be copied before release.

  1. It finds your level before it teaches

    A short adaptive placement measures what you already know, so you start where you are. Re-teaching what someone has mastered wastes their time and can even get in the way of learning.

    [1] [2]

  2. You master each step before moving on

    Topics are proven before the next ones build on them, and gaps are repaired as they appear. Mastery learning and one-to-one tutoring are among the most effective approaches ever measured.

    [3] [4]

  3. You learn by remembering, not rereading

    Lessons and practice ask you to recall answers, with feedback, instead of reading them again. Retrieving knowledge makes it last far longer than restudying it.

    [5] [6] [7] [8]

  4. Skills come back just before you would forget them

    Savant tracks each skill and brings it back for review on a schedule set for you, sooner when it starts to slip. Spacing practice out, timed to the person, is one of the most reliable findings in learning research.

    [9] [10] [11] [12]

  5. Practice mixes topics together

    Once a topic has been taught, practice mixes it with earlier ones, so you learn to tell problems apart and choose the right method.

    [13] [14]

  6. Clear explanations and useful feedback

    New ideas start with worked examples, and every answer explains why it is right or wrong.

    [15] [16] [17]

  7. It adapts to how you are actually doing

    Savant shapes each lesson from your own answers and interests, like a personal tutor. It does not sort people into "learning styles", because research does not support teaching to them.

    [18] [19] [20]

  8. Games where they help, in moderation

    Learning games appear where they support practice, not as a reward that replaces the reason to learn. Research finds games help, with mixed results for points and badges, so Savant uses them with care.

    [21] [22] [23] [24]

What research can and cannot show. Research tells us which methods work. Whether Savant puts them together in the best way for each person is something we measure with real learners, which is what the beta is for.
